10 minutes of presentation, 5 minutes of questions. Written so that either member can
deliver any section; the split in `contribution_and_review.md` is the preferred one, and
the fallback if only one member can present is marked at the end.

All figures below were produced by the pipeline on its last full run and are reproducible

## Slide 1 — Problem and scenario (45 seconds)

A mining operator runs pits, a processing plant, tailings and a remote camp through
networked equipment, industrial IoT sensors and remote access. The security question the
project answers is: **which remote users, IIoT devices or network events should be
investigated first, and on what evidence?**

Say early that the decision we support is a triage decision, not an alert count. Everything
in the console ends in an action for a named account or asset.

## Slide 2 — Data sources and their status (1 minute 15)

Five source families, 13 files, **635,833 records**.

| Source | Records | Status |
|---|---:|---|
| ToN_IoT IIoT telemetry, 7 sensors | 401,119 | real research data (UNSW Canberra) |
| ToN_IoT Zeek network flows | 211,043 | real research data |
| Remote-access log | 22,686 | synthetic, seeded generator |
| MITRE ATT&CK for ICS descriptions | 357 | real public data |
| Maintenance and incident tickets | 320 | synthetic |
| Asset inventory / user directory | 194 / 114 | synthetic |

**Say the honesty statement here, not later.** Three sentences:

> ToN_IoT is a research testbed, not mining equipment — it stands in for mining IIoT and OT
> behaviour, and that substitution is documented. The access log and tickets are synthetic,
> generated by rules you can read in the script. The four abnormal access patterns in them
> were injected by us on purpose, so when we say the model detected them, we mean it found
> what we planted — not that we caught a real attacker.

Getting this in early removes the obvious attack line in Q&A and makes the rest of the talk
easier to trust.

## Slide 3 — Pipeline and data quality (1 minute)

Eighteen stages from raw files to compliance checklist, every stage runnable on its own,
**37 logged cleaning actions**, provenance hashes on every raw file, 16 test cases.

Two decisions worth naming aloud:

- Zeek writes `-` where a field does not apply. Left as text it becomes one of the
  strongest predictors in the model — an entirely artificial one. It is treated as missing.
- The flow dataset contains **20,569 exact duplicate rows**. They are removed *before* the
  train/test split. Split first and the same record lands on both sides and inflates every
  score.

## Slide 4 — Supervised detection (1 minute 30)

Held-out results, network intrusion detection:

| Model | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Logistic regression | 0.9934 | 0.9493 | 0.9708 |
| Decision tree | 0.9959 | 0.9942 | 0.9951 |
| **Random Forest** | 0.9987 | 0.9987 | **0.9987** |

Attack-family identification: macro-F1 **0.9645**; the weakest class is MITM at 0.743 with
only 312 test examples — a data-volume limit, and say so rather than hiding it.

Modbus telemetry (the OT-specific case): Random Forest **0.9887** against logistic
regression **0.5825**. That gap is the point: manipulated register values are not high or
low in a straight line, they are abnormal in combination.

## Slide 5 — Anomaly detection, including the negative result (1 minute 30)

This is the slide that separates the project from a tutorial.

- Isolation Forest on the flows, fully unsupervised: **ROC-AUC 0.279** — worse than
  guessing.
- Why: **78 per cent of that capture is attack traffic**, so "rare" means ordinary office
  traffic. The method was not broken; the assumption behind it was.
- Refit on known-good traffic only and score novelty against it: **ROC-AUC 0.716**.
- On the access data, where the assumption holds, the same method gets **ROC-AUC 0.997**
  and flags 2 per cent of user-days. Local Outlier Factor on the same features gets 0.501 —
  no better than chance, because the injected behaviour is globally unusual, not locally
  unusual.

The operational lesson: anomaly detection in OT needs a trusted baseline window, not
"whatever is rare today".

## Slide 6 — Access analytics and the investigation (1 minute 30)

Seven behaviour rules, **176 alerts**, every alert carrying its evidence event IDs.

| Rule | Alerts | Precision against injected behaviour |
|---|---:|---:|
| R1 failed-login burst | 11 | 1.00 |
| R2 password spray | 1 | 1.00 |
| R3 impossible travel | 3 | 1.00 |
| R4 out-of-hours privileged action | 101 | **0.06** |
| R5 first-seen country | 41 | 1.00 |
| R6 dormant account reactivated | 1 | 1.00 |
| R7 privileged session without MFA | 18 | 1.00 |

Do not skip R4. Say: it fires 101 times at 6 per cent precision because OT staff work
shifts, so "out of hours" is normal for them. It is kept deliberately as the tuning
counter-example, and it is logged as an open defect with a fix planned (role-aware hours).

The case the evidence ranking selected: account `vend069`, a vendor technician — **16
privileged actions, every one of them without MFA**, on OT targets, from a foreign address,
across four nights, touching **8 assets**. The timeline has **202 rows**; 11 of them are
marked CONTEXT ONLY because they come from the ToN_IoT capture and share only the calendar
window. Point at that column: it is the difference between correlation and a claim.

## Slide 7 — Simulation (1 minute)

Five control scenarios, 5,000 Monte Carlo runs each, over a graph built from the asset
inventory.

| Scenario | P(reach OT) | P(reach a critical OT asset) | Mean assets compromised |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Current controls | 0.541 | 0.539 | 29.6 |
| OT segmentation | 0.344 | 0.316 | 18.5 |
| MFA on remote and privileged sessions | 0.190 | 0.189 | 10.1 |
| Detection and isolation | 0.510 | 0.498 | 18.0 |
| All three | 0.100 | 0.082 | 3.0 |

Two caveats to say out loud: only the foothold probability is measured from our data, the
rest are stated assumptions; and the ranking holds across a ±40 per cent sensitivity sweep,
which is why we present the comparison and not a forecast.

## Slide 8 — Risk, robustness and the prototype (1 minute 30)

Risk score: six readable components, bands set as an alerting budget (top 0.5 per cent
critical), **ROC-AUC 0.988** against the injected behaviour. The next-day early-warning
model reaches only **0.528** — reported as a negative result rather than tuned until it
looked better.

Robustness, four test families:

- 50 per cent of collected fields lost: **F1 −0.226**. Telemetry completeness is itself a
  security control.
- 30 per cent of training labels flipped: −0.04.
- Padding and slowing attack flows: almost no effect — because the model relies on protocol
  fields, not volume. That makes our evasion test the weak one, and it is on the backlog.

Then demo the console: overview → watchlist → the `vend069` case → simulation tab.

## Slide 9 — Close (30 seconds)

What we would tell the mine's management: enforce MFA on remote and privileged sessions
first, then segment IT-OT to OT traffic, then detection and isolation. Under the model,
that takes the chance of an intrusion reaching the OT zone from 54 per cent to 10 per cent.

## Question-and-answer preparation

**"Your F1 is 0.998 — is that not overfitting?"**
Duplicates were removed before the split, selection used cross-validation on the training
data only, and the test set was scored once. The score is high because ToN_IoT is a clean
laboratory capture, not because the evaluation is loose. On live mining traffic it would
drop, and the robustness tests show by how much under different failure modes.

**"Why is your unsupervised model worse than random?"**
Because attacks are the majority class in that capture, so outliers are the normal traffic.
We kept the result and showed the fix — refit on a clean baseline, ROC-AUC 0.716.

**"How much of your data is real?"**
612,519 of 635,833 records are real research data. The identity/access source, the tickets
and the asset context are synthetic, because the charter forbids real credentials and no
authentic mining access log was available. The generator is in the repository and the four
injected patterns are recorded in a ground-truth column.

**"If the access data is synthetic, what have you actually proved?"**
That the pipeline detects behaviour whose ground truth we know, with measured precision and
recall, and that the same methods run end to end over half a million records. We do not
claim to have detected a real intrusion, and no product in the repository says we did.

**"Why Random Forest and not deep learning?"**
Flow data is a mixture of skewed numeric measures and categorical protocol fields with
strong interactions, and we need per-feature explanations for an analyst. A tree ensemble
gives both, on 190,000 rows, in under a minute, without scaling assumptions. A neural model
would need more data and give less explanation.

**"What would you do next?"**
Three things, all on the backlog: an evasion test that perturbs the protocol features the
model actually uses; an authentic identity source if one is approved; and live ingestion in
the console instead of pre-computed outputs.

**"Who did what?"**
Answer honestly and specifically, using the module ownership table. Both members must be
able to explain any module, including the partner's.
